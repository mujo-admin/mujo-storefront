The principles

Why these forms. Why these doses.

Three rules govern every ingredient decision at Mujo. They're not marketing copy, they're the reason the formula looks the way it does.

01 / Fruiting body only
Never mycelium on grain.
Most mushroom coffees use mycelium (the root system) grown on sterilized oats or sorghum. The resulting powder is mostly starch with trace bioactive compounds. Mujo uses only fruiting body, the part of the mushroom people have actually eaten for thousands of years, and tests every batch for beta-glucans and triterpenes.
02 / Disclosure where it matters
Clinical ingredients named, supporting ones categorized.
For ingredients where precise dose drives the outcome. KSM-66® at its 300mg clinical trial dose. Bioavailable Curcumin in its solid-lipid form. Rhodiola Rosea standardized to 5% rosavins and 1.5% salidrosides. We disclose exactly what's in the formula. For the supporting cast, we share category totals and sourcing, and let the recipe stay the recipe. Proprietary blends hide what's in them. Craftsmanship doesn't show its exact margins.
03 / Branded when it matters
The research uses the brand.
For ingredients where form matters, generic isn't the same compound. KSM-66® Ashwagandha is the form behind the Chandrasekhar 2012 RCT cortisol-rhythm research. Bioavailable Curcumin is the solid-lipid form clinically shown to cross the blood-brain barrier and achieve 285x greater free-curcumin bioavailability. Rhodiola Rosea is sourced as a wild-crafted Altai Mountain extract at premium standardization. Paying for the research means paying for the specific form the research validated.

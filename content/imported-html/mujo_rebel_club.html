From the founder

Why a club, and not another newsletter?

Image Placeholder
Kinga · candid
At a padel match, in the kitchen with kids, or at a Rebel Club gathering. Not a headshot.

I started Mujo because I was tired of being marketed to. Tired of being told what I already knew wasn't working was, in fact, working. Tired of 50mg ashwagandha in a pink can with the word "calm" on it.

The Rebel Club exists because most of what's called "wellness community" online isn't a community. It's a marketing channel dressed up like one. Engagement funnels. Affiliate schemes. Influencers paid to post the exact same wellness routine three Tuesdays a month.

I don't want that. And if you've found your way here, you probably don't either.

So this is what the Rebel Club actually is: a small, deliberately-slow newsletter I write twice a month. The real questions I'm wrestling with: about my nervous system, my kids, my training, my business. The research I'm reading. The formulations I'm thinking through. The things I'd tell a friend over coffee, not the things I'd put in an ad.

Members get early access to everything we launch. Member-only essays. The occasional drop designed just for rebels. And one day, in-person gatherings, starting locally and expanding as the club grows.

That's it. That's the whole pitch.

The honest part

A few things the Rebel Club is not.

Not
A daily email machine. You'll hear from us twice a month, not twice a day. Your inbox is already busy enough. We won't add noise to it.
Not
A sponsored content factory. We've never run a sponsored post and we don't plan to start. If we recommend something, it's because we actually use it.
Not
A Facebook group or Discord. We're not trying to replace your friends. We're trying to respect your time.
Is
A slow, thoughtful letter from a founder who reads a lot. Who'd rather be interesting once a fortnight than forgettable every day.
